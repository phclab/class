Passive cable · RC circuit

Myelin Cable Explorer

by Po-Han Chiang @ NYCU · LinkedIn

An axon behaves like a leaky, capacitive cable: a chain of RC circuits joined by the resistance of the axoplasm. Wrapping it in myelin raises the membrane resistance and lowers its capacitance. Clamp one end at +20 mV, either held or as a brief pulse, and watch how far, and how fast, the voltage spreads with and without myelin.

The cable as a chain of RC circuits

Each segment has a membrane resistance rm in parallel with a membrane capacitance cm, and segments are joined by the axial resistance ri. With n myelin wraps the membrane stack is 2n + 1 membranes thick: rm goes up and cm goes down by that factor, while ri is unchanged.

Cable constants

QuantityUnmyelinatedMyelinatedRatio
λ = √(rm / ri) = √(Rm·d / 4Ri)    τ = rm·cm = Rm·Cm    spread speed ≈ 2λ / τ

Specific values: Rm = 5,000 Ω·cm², Cm = 1 µF/cm² and Ri = 100 Ω·cm for the bare membrane; myelin multiplies Rm and divides Cm by 2n + 1. The cable is 5 mm long with sealed ends, solved with 250 compartments and an implicit time step of 0.02 ms.

Why myelin works: less leak

Current that flows out through the membrane is lost before it can travel further down the axon. Myelin makes the membrane resistance much larger, so less current leaks out and the voltage decays over a longer distance. The length constant λ grows with the square root of rm.

Why myelin works: less charge to move

Before the voltage can rise at a point, the membrane capacitance there has to be charged. Thicker insulation means a smaller capacitance, so the same axial current raises the voltage faster. Because rm rises and cm falls by the same factor, τ stays the same while λ gets longer, and the signal spreads faster.

What this model leaves out

  • It is purely passive: there are no voltage-gated channels, so the signal only decays.
  • In a real axon, gaps in the myelin (nodes of Ranvier) regenerate the action potential. The passive spread shown here is what carries the signal from one node to the next, which is why conduction looks like it jumps (saltatory conduction).
  • Without myelin, a thicker axon also helps, but speed only grows with √d. Myelinated fibres get much more speed per unit of diameter.
