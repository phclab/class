Dopamine

by Po-Han Chiang @ NYCU

Slow · Modulatory

Only a few hundred thousand neurons in the human midbrain make dopamine, yet they reach most of the forebrain. Dopamine rarely tells a neuron to fire. It tells a circuit how much the last event mattered, and which synapses should keep the change.

Tyrosine → L-DOPA → dopamine. Add one more enzyme, dopamine β-hydroxylase, and the same cell makes noradrenaline instead; add another, and adrenaline. The pathways are the same chemistry stopped at different points.

The five steps at a dopamine synapse

  1. Synthesis. Tyrosine hydroxylase makes L-DOPA; this step is rate-limiting and is itself inhibited by dopamine, so the terminal regulates its own supply. DOPA decarboxylase then finishes the job in one step.
  2. Loading. VMAT2 packs dopamine into vesicles. Amphetamine starts here, collapsing the vesicular gradient so dopamine floods the cytoplasm, and the dopamine then leaves through a reversed DAT.
  3. Release. Most release is from varicosities along the axon rather than classical synapses. Tonic firing at a few hertz sets a background level; brief bursts produce transients that last a second or so.
  4. Receptor binding. All five receptors are metabotropic. D1-like receptors raise cAMP and make the direct pathway more excitable; D2-like receptors lower it and quieten the indirect pathway.
  5. Clearance. DAT takes dopamine back; MAO breaks down what reaches the cytoplasm — mainly MAO-A inside the terminal, MAO-B in the surrounding glia — and COMT methylates the rest inside neighbouring cells. In prefrontal cortex DAT is sparse, so COMT matters much more there than in striatum.

Reward prediction error

What the bursts encode

Dopamine neurons do not fire for reward as such. They fire when the outcome is better than predicted, stay at baseline when it matches the prediction, and pause when it is worse. As an animal learns, the burst moves from the reward itself to the earliest cue that predicts it.

Why that is useful

This is the error term of temporal-difference learning, broadcast to wide territories, though the signal is not the same everywhere. Synapses that were recently active and receive the burst are strengthened, which is how a circuit can learn from an outcome that arrives seconds after the action.

The same logic makes the addiction story concrete: drugs that raise dopamine directly produce a prediction error that never shrinks with experience, so the learning signal keeps reinforcing the behaviour.

D2 receptors have much higher affinity for dopamine than D1. Tonic, low-level dopamine therefore acts mostly through D2, while only phasic bursts reach D1 — one receptor system listening for the background, the other for the news. It is a useful first approximation rather than a clean split.

When it goes wrong

  • Parkinson's disease. Loss of nigrostriatal terminals first and cell bodies later; motor symptoms begin only after most of the striatal dopamine is gone, because what survives compensates.
  • Schizophrenia. Too much subcortical dopamine alongside too little in prefrontal cortex; treating one can worsen the other.
  • Addiction. Drugs bypass the prediction-error logic and reinforce behaviour directly.
  • Impulse-control disorders. D3-preferring agonists used in Parkinson's disease can trigger gambling or compulsive shopping — the treatment reproducing the disorder's mirror image.
