Messengers Without a Vesicle

by Po-Han Chiang @ NYCU

Retrograde · On demand · Diffusible

The five-step scheme assumes a molecule is made, stored, released forwards and cleared. Four important messengers break at least one of those rules: ATP is stored and released normally but turns into a different signal, adenosine, once outside; the others are made only when needed, one of them travelling backwards across the synapse and another passing straight through membranes as a gas.

Retrograde signalling, drawn

Endocannabinoids are how a postsynaptic cell tells its inputs to quieten down. Strong depolarisation raises Ca²⁺, 2-arachidonoylglycerol is cut out of the membrane, it crosses the cleft backwards and activates CB1 on the terminal, which closes Ca²⁺ channels and cuts release for five to twenty seconds. Physiologists call it depolarisation-induced suppression of inhibition, or of excitation, depending on which terminal carries CB1.

Purines: the transmitter that is also the fuel

ATP outside the cell

ATP is co-packaged with classical transmitters and released with them, then acts on P2X receptors, which are ionotropic and calcium permeable, and on P2Y receptors, which are G-protein coupled. Microglia are exquisitely sensitive to it: extracellular ATP is one of the main signals of injury.

Adenosine and sleep pressure

Ectonucleotidases strip the phosphates, and the adenosine that results acts on A1 receptors to inhibit release and on A2A receptors in the striatum. Adenosine accumulates during waking and is one of the substances that make sleep pressure build. Caffeine blocks both receptors — it does not add energy, it removes the brake.

Why this matters for the rest of the course

  • Feedback needs a backward channel. Classical transmission is one-way; endocannabinoids give the postsynaptic cell a way to set its own input strength.
  • Not every signal needs a receptor on the surface. Nitric oxide crosses membranes and acts on soluble guanylyl cyclase inside the target cell, raising cGMP, which is why it cannot be confined to one synapse.
  • Glia are participants. ATP, adenosine and D-serine are released or controlled by astrocytes; how much of each comes from glia rather than from neurons is still argued, but the synapse is clearly not just two cells.
  • Drugs that hit a diffuse system hit everything. Rimonabant and the first-generation antihistamines make the same point from opposite directions.
